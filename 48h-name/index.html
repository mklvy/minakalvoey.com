I Say Your Name

Mina Amiri Kalvøy, 2025

I’m in a cafeteria. I recently took a number of super recogniser tests and received a record of achievement for being in the top 12% of super recognisers in the world. A super recogniser is someone with significantly-better-than average face recognition ability, as the name might suggest. People with this ability are often headhunted to work for the police or security services, which is not something I aspire to, so I’m trying to keep a low profile. Now, I’m wondering if there is a record of achievement for people with a better-than-average-recognisable face as well?

“I know you!” The woman serving me Swedish meatballs exclaims, before she sings a full verse of the song at me.

*

I’m in a club in a capital city. I feel like everyone is looking at me, as if I’ve done something great, as if I’m amazing. My cheeks are warm, I feel like smiling, like I need to meet everyone’s eyes and say “Thank you! Thank you so much!” I see myself from the outside. I see us from the outside. I feel powerful. In the line at the bar as I’m waiting for my beer, a hand slowly strokes my head before a man leans closer and whispers in my ear:

“You look like a million dollars…like her”

*

I’m at the pharmacy. A couple of days before that, I was collecting thousands of short hairs from the bathroom sink as I had been doing for the past 9 years. You came and stood behind me, and I looked up in the mirror. “Wouldn’t it be nice to grow your hair out now? To have a bit of a change like you mentioned?” I looked down at the white porcelain sink covered in short hairs, then back in the mirror. “I don’t know who I’d be, then,” I answered, her eyes looking back at me through mine.

“You know who you look like?” The man at the pharmacy asks while holding my cyst cream, his fist tightening around it in excitement.
We say her name in unison.

*

I’m in a park one windy afternoon. She’s on my mind. The night before, as I walked past a dark shop window on the street, I sensed a reflection following along. I stopped in front of the window, and tried to make out if the reflection would move on its own, I felt embarrassed when it didn’t. An early morning not long after, I saw a familiar bare head in the midst of a crowd of people. In the backseat of a taxi on my way home from a restaurant, instead of thinking about him, I saw her eyes again looking back at me, this time in the rearview mirror.

“You’ve got that look, love!” The man sitting on a bench in the park swings his beer at me in a cheer as I pass him.

*

It’s evening, I’m in my room with the door closed. Candles flicker in a circle around me as I stand in front of the full-length mirror, looking myself up and down. I turn my head to the right. To the left. Chin up, chin down. Who will I be, then? I look myself in the eyes, and say her name three times.

I close my eyes…

I take a breath…
